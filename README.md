# Bachelor Thesis
## Abstract
The formal loop space is an algebraic formalism encoding the formal properties of classical infinite dimensional phase spaces. It is widely used in the study classical and quantum field Hamiltonian and integrable systems and it focuses on symmetries and conserved quantities without any recourse to functional analysis. This thesis introduces the student to this formalism, making it precise and introducing a notion of Poisson structure on the formal infinite dimensional phase space. Several directions can be developed, from exploring celebrated examples of integrable systems to classifying local Poisson structures. We have chosen to focus the attention on the problem of finding conserved quantities.
## Extended abstract
The formal loop space is the space whose ring of functions is the space of local functionals. This ring is a completion of the quotient of the ring of differential polynomials (polynomials in a field u and its derivatives $u_x, u_{xx}, ...,$ interpreted as formal variables) by the action of the $x$-derivative operator. This simple algebraic construction encodes the operation on integration by parts for functions of a field with periodic or fast-decaying boundary conditions, and does so without ever mentioning functional spaces. On this ring of functions one can introduce derivations to be interpreted as vector fields on the underlying infinite dimensional space and they will correspond to evolutionary PDEs, and even Poisson brackets to that it makes sense to study Hamiltonian systems in the field $u$. This formalism is widely used by the integrable systems community when the interest is in the algebra of symmetries of a given dynamical system and not on its explicit solutions. It can be used for quantization, offering a particularly clean approach to quantum field theory.
